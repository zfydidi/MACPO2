\title{Conflict-Gated Communication for Distributed Black-Box Optimization:\\When to Negotiate Under a Communication Budget}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Network-based distributed optimization (NDO) with mixed private and shared variables must repeatedly coordinate neighbors on shared dimensions, yet always-on negotiation wastes communication when local conflict is weak or stale. This paper treats \emph{communication timing}, the decision of whether to open the negotiation channel at each outer loop, as a first-class problem rather than a fixed rule inside one algorithm. We propose \emph{conflict-triggered communication}. A cheap online conflict proxy triggers negotiation only when inconsistency exceeds a phase-relative baseline, and a fail-safe period guarantees non-zero communication so consensus references do not freeze. The policy is instantiated on the MACPO penalty-negotiation backbone and tested on the MACPO NDO benchmark (F1--F18), periodic-communication baselines, gate ablations, MACPO-style dispatch simulators, and IEEE transmission-network pilots. Conflict gating cuts negotiation trigger rates from always-on to a small fraction of outer loops while preserving or improving final fitness under identical evaluation budgets. Penalty-controller choice (RL, EMA, or fixed schedules) is secondary once gating is fixed. The same mechanism transfers to communication-constrained economic dispatch.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
distributed black-box optimization, communication timing, event-triggered coordination, conflict gating, network-based optimization, economic dispatch
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Network-based distributed optimization (NDO) models systems such as robotics~\cite{ref_dopt_robotics_2024}, smart grids~\cite{ref_dopt_smartgrid_2024}, wireless sensor networks~\cite{ref_dopt_wsn_2023}, and the Internet of Vehicles~\cite{ref_dopt_iov_2024}, in which many agents on a graph optimize a global objective through local information and neighbor interaction~\cite{ref_decentralized_review_2023}. A defining feature is the mixture of private variables and shared edge-coupled variables---as in transportation flow assignment~\cite{ref_ndo_traffic_2022}, electric dispatching~\cite{ref_ndo_dispatch_2024}, distributed state estimation~\cite{ref_ndo_stateest_2024}, and related networked control problems~\cite{ref_ndo_netcontrol_2024}. When objectives are black-box and only locally evaluable, coordination must proceed without analytic gradients, through repeated local search and neighbor negotiation on shared dimensions.

Penalty-based cooperative evolution, exemplified by MACPO \cite{ref_macpo}, is a practical NDO backbone: each agent optimizes a penalized local objective and negotiates with neighbors to align shared variables. MACPO opens communication on nearly every outer loop. When conflict is weak, this cost may not improve shared-variable consistency or final fitness. The bottleneck is therefore not only \emph{how} to negotiate, but \emph{when} negotiation is worth its communication and evaluation cost.

This paper addresses the \textbf{communication-timing problem in distributed black-box optimization}. At each outer iteration, should agents open the negotiation channel? We answer with \textbf{conflict-triggered communication}, a rule-based gate driven by an online conflict proxy, a relative threshold, and a fail-safe silent bound. The gate is orthogonal to the specific penalty or local optimizer. MACPO serves as the reference instantiation and engineering platform, not as the conceptual endpoint.

Three research lines motivate this formulation. In communication-efficient distributed optimization, Local SGD and FedAvg reduce gradient-exchange frequency while retaining convergence under smoothness assumptions \cite{ref_stich_local_sgd_2019,ref_mcmahan_fedavg_2017,ref_koloskova_decsgd_2020}. In event-triggered and self-triggered control, agents communicate when a local error signal crosses a threshold rather than at every clock tick \cite{ref_rabbat_nowak_2004}. In distributed black-box NDO, MACPO \cite{ref_macpo}, MASOIE \cite{ref_masoie_2024}, and MAES-CCSA \cite{ref_maes_ccsa_2025} adapt coordination schedules but do not jointly formalize \emph{when}-to-communicate as an explicit decision with a controllable communication--quality trade-off. Our contribution fills that gap for penalty-negotiation NDO.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Problem formulation.} Communication timing in NDO is cast as a per-iteration binary scheduling decision under a communication budget or fitness tolerance (Section~\ref{sec:timing_problem}).
\item \textbf{Conflict-triggered gate.} A three-layer gate (minimum interval, relative conflict threshold, fail-safe bound) with a fail-safe consensus-drift bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe}) and an empirically validated low-conflict skip criterion (Section~\ref{sec:structural_results}).
\item \textbf{Empirical validation on three axes.} Route~1 covers benchmark and mechanism studies on F1--F18 with periodic and gate ablations. Route~2 covers MACPO-style dispatch and IEEE pilots under bandwidth-limited coordination. Route~3 reports that adaptive penalty control is a plug-in interface, not the dominant gain (Sections~\ref{sec:experiments}--\ref{sec:applications}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}\label{sec:related}

They are less directly applicable to black-box NDO with explicit shared-variable negotiation \cite{ref_back_book,ref_blackbox_tracking_2022}. Cooperative coevolution and penalty methods offer a gradient-free path \cite{ref_potter_dejong,ref_cc_dist_accel_2024,ref_macpo} but typically treat communication as fixed.

\section{NDO Formulation}\label{sec:problem}
Consider $n$ agents on an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ with global decision vector $\mathbf{X}=[x_1,\ldots,x_D]$. For agent $i$,
\begin{equation}
X_i = \mathrm{col}\{x_d\}_{d \in \Theta_i}, \quad \Theta_i = I_i \cup \bigcup_{j \in N_i} I_{i,j}
\label{eq:local_variable_set}
\end{equation}
where $I_i$ indexes private variables on vertex $i$, $I_{i,j}$ indexes shared variables on edge $(i,j)$, and $N_i$ is the neighbor set. The global objective is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^D} F(\mathbf{X}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(\mathbf{X}_i)
\label{eq:global_objective}
\end{equation}
subject to $x_i^d = x_j^d$ for all $d\in I_{i,j}$ and $(i,j)\in E$.

\section{The Communication-Timing Problem}\label{sec:timing_problem}
Let $t=1,2,\ldots,T$ index outer iterations. At each $t$, the system chooses a gate $g^t\in\{0,1\}$. If $g^t=1$, agents enter the negotiation branch. If $g^t=0$, they skip negotiation and continue local search while refreshing consensus references. MACPO fixes $g^t\equiv 1$. The communication-timing problem is to design a policy $\pi$ such that $g^t=\pi(\mathcal{H}^t)$, where $\mathcal{H}^t$ contains observable local history (local best, consensus reference, conflict proxy, time since last communication).

Two equivalent objectives capture the engineering trade-off.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Budget form.} Minimize $F(\mathbf{X}^T)$ subject to average trigger rate $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_t g^t \le B$.
\item \textbf{Tolerance form.} Minimize $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ subject to $F(\mathbf{X}^T)$ not worse than an always-on baseline by more than tolerance $\delta$.
\end{itemize}
Black-box NDO lacks convexity, so we do not claim global convergence theorems. Instead, we provide a fail-safe drift bound on reference staleness between communications and an empirical skip criterion validated in Section~\ref{sec:exp_q2} (Sections~\ref{sec:gate_design} and~\ref{sec:structural_results}).

\section{Conflict-Triggered Communication}\label{sec:gate_design}
\subsection{Gate Design}
Conflict gating decides \emph{whether} to open communication before paying negotiation cost. For each active overlap dimension $d$, let $x_d$ be the local best and $r_d$ the consensus reference on the search range $R_d$. The per-dimension conflict is the exponentially smoothed normalized gap
\begin{equation}
c_d^t = \beta c_d^{t-1} + (1-\beta)\frac{|x_d^t-r_d^t|}{R_d}, \qquad \beta=0.7,
\label{eq:ci_dim}
\end{equation}
and the agent conflict proxy is the mean $\mathrm{CI}_i^t=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_i|}\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_i} c_d^t$ over active overlap dimensions $\mathcal{D}_i$. High $\mathrm{CI}$ indicates disagreement likely to influence subsequent local optimization, whereas low $\mathrm{CI}$ carries limited expected negotiation value. This follows the role of the gradient-based conflict index in \cite{ref_macpo} but uses position gaps only. The phase baseline is an EMA update $\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t = \gamma \hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^{t-1} + (1-\gamma)\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ with $\gamma=0.9$. The local gate is
\begin{equation}
g_i^t=\mathbf{1}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t\right]
\label{eq:local_gate}
\end{equation}
with minimum-interval constraint $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t$ ($t-t_{\mathrm{last\_comm}}\ge \Delta_{\min}$), threshold constraint
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t\!:\ \mathrm{CI}_i^t > \lambda\,\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t\ \text{or}\ t-t_{\mathrm{last\_comm}}\ge K$,
and phase-sampling constraint $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t\!:\ u_i^t < p_{\mathrm{phase}}^t$. Global synchronization uses $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=\max_i g_i^t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_gate}). Algorithm~\ref{alg:ci_gating} summarizes the procedure and Fig.~\ref{fig:gate_policy} shows the per-agent decision flow. Table~\ref{tab:gate_defaults} lists the headline default values used in the F1--F18 experiments unless an ablation caption states otherwise.

\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=\max_i g_i^t
\label{eq:global_gate}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \footnotesize
  \caption{Conflict-Gated Communication with Phase Sampling}
  \label{alg:ci_gating}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \For{each outer iteration $t$}
    \State Compute $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ and update $\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$
    \State Check $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t$, $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t$ (relative threshold or fail-safe $K$), and $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t$
    \State $g_i^t\gets \mathbf{1}[\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t]$
    \State $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t\gets \max_i g_i^t$
    \If{$g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=0$} \State Skip negotiation and refresh consensus reference
    \Else \State Enter negotiation branch \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

At iteration $t$, agent $i$ opens its local gate only if interval, relative-threshold (or fail-safe), and phase-sampling checks in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_gate} all pass. Otherwise it skips negotiation and refreshes consensus references (Fig.~\ref{fig:gate_policy}).

\subsection{Structural Bound and Empirical Criterion}\label{sec:structural_results}
We do not prove bounded per-loop drift for MACPO local search. Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe} instead treats bounded normalized shared-gap growth during silent loops as an engineering assumption and shows what fail-safe timing guarantees \emph{if} that assumption holds. Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_drift} reports that observed per-loop drift on archived F1--F6 headline runs stays in a narrow band, supporting the assumption empirically rather than analytically.

\begin{proposition}[Fail-safe consensus-drift bound]\label{prop:failsafe}
Suppose that between any two consecutive communication events, each normalized shared gap $e_d^t=|x_d^t-r_d^t|/R_d$ increases by at most $\varepsilon_d\ge 0$ on every silent outer loop with $g^t=0$, and that the relative-threshold rule includes fail-safe $t-t_{\mathrm{last\_comm}}\ge K$. Then any silent stretch has length $\tau=t-t_{\mathrm{last\_comm}}\le K-1$ before communication is forced, and
\begin{equation}
e_d^{t_{\mathrm{last\_comm}}+\tau} \le e_d^{t_{\mathrm{last\_comm}}} + (K-1)\,\varepsilon_{\max},
\qquad \varepsilon_{\max}=\max_d \varepsilon_d.
\label{eq:drift_bound}
\end{equation}
If agent $i$ uses a separable conflict penalty $\alpha_i\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_i} e_d^t$, the incremental penalty inflation attributable to silent drift is at most $\alpha_i(K-1)\varepsilon_{\max}|\mathcal{D}_i|$. Fail-safe therefore limits both reference staleness and penalty distortion between negotiations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
This is a structural fail-safe bound under assumed drift, not a MACPO convergence theorem. A mechanical corollary is that fail-safe-only scheduling enforces $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}\ge 1/K$. With minimum interval and phase sampling (Table~\ref{tab:gate_defaults}), the observed F3--F18 trigger rate is about $1/12$ (Table~\ref{tab:comm_rate_f1_f18}), above that floor because fail-safe, interval, and phase paths interact.
\end{remark}

\begin{empiricalcriterion}[Low-conflict skip rule]\label{crit:skip}
At outer loop $t$, skip negotiation when for all active agents (i) $\mathrm{CI}_i^t \le \lambda\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$, (ii) overlap variables have moved little since $t_{\mathrm{last\_comm}}$, and (iii) fail-safe has not fired. This is the relative-threshold branch of Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_gate}. It is an engineering skip test motivated by event-triggered coordination, not a guarantee that one MACPO round would leave $f_{\mathrm{pure}}$ unchanged.
\end{empiricalcriterion}
\begin{remark}
We validate this criterion with gate ablations (Table~\ref{tab:gate_cost_stats}), CI-conditioned trigger statistics (Fig.~\ref{fig:ci_bin_trigger}), and communication summaries (Table~\ref{tab:f1f6_comm_eva}). On F1/F2/F5, gated policies occupy a better region of the communication-budget frontier than fixed-interval schedules (Fig.~\ref{fig:comm_fitness_pareto}). On F1--F6, trigger probability falls from the lowest conflict quintile to the second and third quintiles, consistent with skipping low-informativeness rounds while fail-safe and interval paths keep Q1 low.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Expected Communication--Quality Trade-off}
Under gate $g^t$, expected negotiation cost scales as $\mathcal{O}(I\,\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}\,\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}\sum_{(i,j)}|L_{i,j}|)$ versus $\mathcal{O}(I\sum_{(i,j)}|L_{i,j}|)$ for always-on MACPO (Section~\ref{sec:complexity}). Knobs $(\lambda,K,p_{\mathrm{phase}})$ trace a controllable trade-off surface between $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ and terminal $F$.

\section{MACPO Instantiation}\label{sec:method}

If $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=1$, it negotiates on shared variables via Nash-style competition and two-sided perturbation checking (Algorithm~\ref{alg:negotiation_consistency}). Fig.~\ref{fig:rl_macpo_architecture} shows the pipeline. \emph{Communication} is the gate decision and \emph{negotiation} is post-gate processing.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \footnotesize
  \caption{Negotiation with Two-Sided Consistency Check (Abstract)}
  \label{alg:negotiation_consistency}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Neighbor pair $(i,j)$, shared set $L_{i,j}$, variable switch $\mathrm{switch}[\cdot]$
  \For{each shared dimension $d\in L_{i,j}$ with $\mathrm{switch}[d]=1$}
    \State Nash-style bilateral comparison on $d$
    \State Two-sided perturbation check and update $\mathrm{switch}[d]$ if consistent
    \State Write accepted values to consensus reference
  \EndFor
  \State Re-evaluate local population
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Optional Modules (Secondary)}\label{sec:optional_modules}
\textbf{Variable selection.} Once communication is granted, shared dimensions can be ranked by importance score $q_d^t$. Low-impact dimensions may be skipped. Ablations show this is auxiliary once gating is active (Section~\ref{sec:q3_modules}).

\textbf{Adaptive penalty interface (Route~3).}\label{sec:penalty_interface}
Penalty strength adapts through a common interface (RL default, EMA, or fixed phase schedules). Under fixed gating, controllers are statistically tied on F3/F5 (Table~\ref{tab:penalty_controller_f3_f5}). RL is retained as a unified default without benchmark-specific schedules, not as a universal superiority claim.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}\label{sec:experiments}
We organize experiments around four questions aligned with the communication-timing formulation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Q1 (Quality):} Does gated MACPO match or improve fitness at the same evaluation budget?
\item \textbf{Q2 (Communication):} Does conflict triggering reduce $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ without sacrificing Q1?
\item \textbf{Q3 (Mechanism):} Is gating necessary? Is RL necessary once gating is fixed?
\item \textbf{Q4 (Transfer):} Does the mechanism transfer to dispatch and grid pilots (Route~2)?
\end{itemize}
Protocol uses the MACPO NDO suite F1--F18 \cite{ref_macpo}, 25 paired runs, LLSO as the primary optimizer and CSO as secondary, and Wilcoxon/Mann--Whitney tests at the 0.05 level. Table columns labeled \textbf{RL-MACPO} denote the archived full stack with conflict gate and RL penalty unless an ablation caption states otherwise.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\section{Complexity Analysis}\label{sec:complexity}
Under always-on MACPO, negotiation scales as $\mathcal{O}(I\sum_{(i,j)}|L_{i,j}|)$ per outer loop. With conflict triggering,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{nego}}^{\mathrm{Gated}}\right]
=
\mathcal{O}\!\left(
I\,\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}\,\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}
\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|L_{i,j}|\,C_f
\right),
\label{eq:nego_complexity}
\end{equation}
where $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ is the average trigger rate and $\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}$ the fraction of shared dimensions actively negotiated. Equation~\eqref{eq:nego_complexity} models the expected execution cost under the empirically observed communication frequency, rather than a worst-case asymptotic complexity bound. Section~\ref{sec:exp_q2} shows $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ drops to a small fraction of outer loops. Gating rather than penalty choice dominates the reduction. The execution-cost model is therefore empirical and relies on the bounded-drift assumption summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_drift}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Route~1: Communication timing as the core insight.}
The half-year engineering effort converges on one mechanism: \emph{negotiation need not occur every outer loop}. The proposed architecture separates \emph{whether to communicate} from \emph{how to negotiate}: conflict gating decides timing, while penalty update remains a plug-in module. In standard MACPO, communicate and negotiate are coupled; here, a gate first asks whether negotiation is warranted and only then invokes the existing MACPO negotiation step. Conflict-triggered communication, with fail-safe bounds, provides a controllable communication--quality trade-off in penalty-negotiation NDO. The conflict proxy $\mathrm{CI}$ operationalizes this idea. High $\mathrm{CI}$ marks disagreement likely to affect subsequent local search, whereas low $\mathrm{CI}$ rounds carry limited expected negotiation value. Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe}, Empirical Criterion~\ref{crit:skip}, CI-bin trigger diagnostics (Fig.~\ref{fig:ci_bin_trigger}), and gate ablations (Table~\ref{tab:gate_cost_stats}) elevate the contribution from ``tuning MACPO'' to ``formalizing and validating a timing policy'' within the event-triggered / communication-efficient distributed optimization spectrum (Table~\ref{tab:positioning}). The particularly large numerical gaps on F9 and F15 mainly reflect unresolved penalty violations in the original MACPO implementation rather than proportional improvements in the underlying objective. This viewpoint is algorithm-agnostic and is therefore applicable beyond MACPO-style penalty negotiation.

\paragraph{Route~2: Grid dispatch as validation domain.}
Dispatch simulators and IEEE pilots show transfer when shared/private structure mirrors MACPO-style NDO under bandwidth limits (Section~\ref{sec:applications}).

\paragraph{Route~3: RL and variable selection honestly demoted.}
Table~\ref{tab:penalty_controller_f3_f5} shows RL, EMA, and fixed schedules are statistically tied once gating is fixed. Variable selection and phase sampling ablations show limited endpoint sensitivity. RL remains a convenient default penalty implementation, not a standalone research claim. The penalty interface also permits future heuristic or model-based controllers without modifying the communication architecture. A genuine RL contribution would target harder timing decisions (who/when to communicate), left as future work (Section~\ref{sec:future_rl}).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Internal comparisons use a common MACPO codebase. MASOIE (Table~\ref{tab:external_masoie}) is cross-paradigm rather than matched-budget, and MAES-CCSA remains unavailable in open source. Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe} assumes bounded per-loop shared drift rather than proving it for MACPO local search. Appendix~\ref{app:empirical_drift} reports the observed drift range on F1--F6. Appendices~\ref{app:rl_controller} and~\ref{app:applications} provide supplementary diagnostics without changing the main conclusions. Empirical Criterion~\ref{crit:skip} is validated statistically, not derived as a MACPO optimality theorem. IEEE~118 shows a detectable cost offset. Asynchronous and time-varying networks remain open.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion}
Rather than proposing another optimizer, this work argues that communication timing itself should be regarded as a first-class design problem in distributed black-box optimization. Conflict-triggered communication (relative conflict proxy, fail-safe bound, and phase sampling) cuts negotiation rates from always-on to a small fraction of outer loops while preserving or improving final fitness on F1--F18 and in dispatch pilots. MACPO is the instantiation platform. The insight belongs to communication-efficient NDO broadly.

Future work includes fuller OPF-constrained dispatch benchmarks (Route~2), learned policies for topology-level timing decisions (Route~3), and matched-budget comparisons once MAES-CCSA code is available.\label{sec:future_rl}
If learning is to justify an ``RL'' label, the decision object must move from scalar penalty ratios to \emph{communication scheduling}---selecting neighbors, rounds, or subgraphs---where hand-crafted rules do not suffice. Penalty control alone does not meet that bar in our data.

\subsection{Training Stability}\label{app:rl_stability}
Across 25 independent runs per benchmark, logged $\alpha$, $\rho$, conflict, and reward traces in Fig.~\ref{fig:rl_traj_metrics} evolve smoothly without run-to-run divergence once gating is active. Reward means plateau after the early evaluation phase. Cross-run standard deviation remains bounded on F1--F6. This supports treating PPO as a stable default penalty updater rather than a fragile, benchmark-tuned add-on.

\section{Additional Application-Case Statistics}\label{app:applications}
Section~\ref{sec:applications} reports headline paired means in Tables~\ref{tab:application_cases} and~\ref{tab:ieee_power_cases}. Table~\ref{tab:application_run_stats} adds run-level paired cost-gap quartiles and min--max ranges (25 runs per case; communication rates summarized by pilot group). Dispatch cases show tight medians on MAED-13 and EV dispatch. Resource scheduling exhibits wider spread because of one MACPO outlier run. IEEE~30 includes two LLSO stagnation outliers, consistent with the 23/25 success count in the main table. Excluding those runs leaves median $\Delta$ near zero. These distributions confirm that communication drops are robust while cost effects remain case-dependent.
