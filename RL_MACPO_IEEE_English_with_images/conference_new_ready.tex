\title{Conflict-Gated Communication for Distributed Black-Box Optimization:\\When to Negotiate Under a Communication Budget}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In physical networked systems such as UAV swarms and vehicular edge computing, inter-agent communication is severely constrained by strict energy and bandwidth budgets. Conventional penalty-based cooperative evolution for network-based distributed optimization (NDO) nevertheless relies on an always-on schedule that forces agents to negotiate shared variables on nearly every iteration, wasting communication even when local states are already aligned. To eliminate this redundant overhead, we propose a conflict-gated distributed optimization architecture that explicitly answers when to negotiate. Instead of opaque learning models for trigger decisions, our approach uses a transparent three-layer gate that compares a local conflict proxy against a phase-relative threshold, supplemented by a fail-safe synchronization bound. Reinforcement learning is strictly decoupled from the gating mechanism and serves only as a plug-in for post-gate penalty adaptation. Evaluations on NDO benchmarks and power dispatch simulators confirm that the proposed architecture slashes negotiation triggers by up to 90\% while preserving or even improving terminal fitness under matched evaluation budgets.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
distributed black-box optimization, communication timing, conflict-gated communication, network-based optimization, penalty-based negotiation, multi-agent co-evolution
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Network-based distributed optimization (NDO) arises in systems where decision making is distributed over interconnected nodes and must be coordinated under local information only~\cite{ref_decentralized_review_2023}. In many real-world physical systems, however, inter-agent communication is severely constrained by limited bandwidth, finite energy budgets, or intermittent connectivity, and the cost of transmission often dominates the cost of local computation.

Three settings illustrate why an always-on negotiation schedule is difficult to sustain in practice. In decentralized task allocation for unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms and mobile robot teams, wireless RF communication can consume orders of magnitude more battery than on-board CPU work~\cite{ref_dopt_robotics_2024}. Forcing every outer loop to synchronize shared plans would drain nodes long before optimization converges; the available communication budget must be spent on iterations where neighbor variables actually conflict. In vehicular edge computing (VEC), vehicles and roadside units (RSUs) move relative to one another quickly, leaving only brief contact windows and limited wireless capacity~\cite{ref_dopt_iov_2024}. Agents cannot afford round-by-round, continuous negotiation over neighborhoods that change from moment to moment. In geographically dispersed power systems and electric-vehicle (EV) charging networks, which we evaluate in Section~\ref{sec:applications}, nodes often coordinate over public wide-area links such as cellular or wired backhaul. Frequent synchronization adds latency, congestion, and recurring data charges~\cite{ref_ndo_dispatch_2024,ref_dopt_smartgrid_2024}. In multi-area economic dispatch or EV charging coordination, neighboring control areas or charging stations need to negotiate mainly when tie-line power or charging schedules conflict, not when local states are already aligned.

NDO formalizes these problems: each agent owns private variables and negotiates edge-coupled shared variables with neighbors, as in transportation flow assignment~\cite{ref_ndo_traffic_2022} and distributed state estimation~\cite{ref_ndo_stateest_2024}. When objectives are black-box and only locally observable, progress depends on alternating local search with neighbor negotiation, but only if communication is spent where it matters. Penalty-based cooperative evolution provides one practical solver on this path~\cite{ref_potter_dejong,ref_cc_dist_accel_2024,ref_masoie_2024,ref_maes_ccsa_2025}. A representative always-on schedule, exemplified by MACPO~\cite{ref_macpo}, triggers negotiation on nearly every outer loop regardless of whether shared variables still disagree. That schedule wastes communication budget on unnecessary rounds when local conflict is already weak or unlikely to improve terminal quality. The central challenge is therefore when to open the communication channel under constraints that rule out continuous synchronization.

Researchers have developed two main lines of methods. Gradient-based schemes such as DGD, ADMM, EXTRA, and Push--Pull~\cite{ref_nedic_subgrad,ref_boyd_admm,ref_shi_extra,ref_pu_pushpull_2021} are effective when gradients or favorable structure are available. Communication-efficient federated learning extends this line by adaptively tuning client synchronization from gradient consistency~\cite{ref_gift_fl_2023} and by pruning model updates in decentralized device-to-device edge networks~\cite{ref_dfl_prune_tnse_2025}. Event-triggered distributed optimization pursues the same goal of fewer transmissions via dynamic error thresholds~\cite{ref_et_nonconvex_tac_2023} and asynchronous, link-resilient triggering under heterogeneous computation~\cite{ref_daaet_tac_2024}, but still exchanges gradients or consensus states rather than scheduling explicit shared-variable negotiation in black-box NDO. For gradient-uncomputable problems, penalty-based cooperative evolution remains the relevant paradigm, yet existing always-on negotiation leaves the communication-timing question open under the physical limits described above.

The first question addressed in this paper is when agents should open the negotiation channel. When conflict is low, local shared values already agree with neighborhood references, and skipping negotiation avoids unnecessary perturbation. For this question we propose conflict-gated communication, a non-RL monitoring layer inserted before neighbor negotiation. Each outer loop, the gate compares the conflict proxy with a phase-relative threshold, enforces a minimum wait of $\Delta_{\min}$ outer loops between triggers, and applies a fail-safe rule that forces synchronization after $K$ silent loops. Fig.~\ref{fig:proposed_architecture}(a) shows where this gate sits in the proposed workflow. An always-on schedule fixes $g_i^t\equiv 1$ on every loop; our design makes $g_i^t$ depend on observable local history instead.

The second question is how to set the penalty weight $\alpha_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective} during long runs. A fixed penalty can over-constrain search in low-conflict phases and under-penalize inconsistency in high-conflict phases. Reinforcement learning (RL) has been used to adapt algorithm parameters online in metaheuristics~\cite{ref_karafotias_param_control,ref_rl_ea_survey_2024,ref_rl_metaheuristic_2023,ref_sutton_barto}. For this question we use RL only in the adaptive penalty-control module in Fig.~\ref{fig:proposed_architecture}(b). The policy observes conflict level and trend and updates the bounded penalty ratio $\rho_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_mapping}. RL does not replace evolutionary local search and does not decide whether to communicate. That separation keeps the communication decision interpretable while still allowing data-driven penalty adaptation.

Section~\ref{sec:experiments} evaluates the proposed architecture against an always-on penalty-negotiation baseline under matched evaluation budgets on synthetic NDO benchmarks, mechanism ablations, and application pilots.

We formulate communication timing in distributed black-box NDO as a per-iteration gate decision under a communication budget, and propose a three-layer conflict gate with explicit monitoring rules. We give the gate a theoretical footing with three guarantees, each paired with a validity experiment: a fail-safe consensus-drift bound, a bounded single-round skip suboptimality controlled by the gate threshold (extended to a cumulative bound over a silent stretch), and a convergence rate for intermittent gated consensus showing that gating at trigger rate $p$ is equivalent to a consensus recursion with effective gain $p\lambda_c$. We present a conflict-gated distributed optimization architecture that schedules neighbor negotiation from local conflict signals, and show through experiments that negotiation triggers fall to a small fraction of outer loops while terminal fitness matches or improves under the same evaluation budget. We further demonstrate that the gate is protocol-agnostic: it plugs unchanged into structurally different negotiation rules in a solver-agnostic sandbox and into the real external-learning solver MASOIE, where it cuts communication substantially while preserving fitness on consensus-tolerant problems. We implement RL-based adaptive penalty control as a modular component that adjusts $\alpha_i$ without replacing local evolutionary search, and verify through ablations that it is secondary to conflict gating.

Section~\ref{sec:related} positions it relative to communication-efficient optimization. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports experimental studies. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.

\section{Network-Based Distributed Optimization}\label{sec:problem}
Network-based distributed optimization (NDO) exists in traffic assignment in transportation networks~\cite{ref_ndo_traffic_2022}, electric dispatching in power systems~\cite{ref_ndo_dispatch_2024}, distributed state estimation~\cite{ref_ndo_stateest_2024}, and related networked control problems~\cite{ref_ndo_netcontrol_2024}. In these scenarios, data and decisions are naturally scattered across network nodes, and some decision variables are shared by neighboring agents. The mathematical definition of NDO is given as follows. Consider $n$ agents on an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ with global decision vector $\mathbf{X}=[x_1,\ldots,x_D]$. For agent $i$,
\begin{equation}
X_i = \mathrm{col}\{x_d\}_{d \in \Theta_i}, \quad \Theta_i = I_i \cup \bigcup_{j \in N_i} I_{i,j}
\label{eq:local_variable_set}
\end{equation}
Here $X_i$ is the subvector agent $i$ can read and update; $\Theta_i$ is its active variable set; $I_i$ indexes private dimensions owned only by agent $i$; $I_{i,j}$ indexes shared dimensions on edge $(i,j)$; and $N_i$ is the neighbor set of agent $i$. The global objective aggregates local black-box costs:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^D} F(\mathbf{X}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(\mathbf{X}_i)
\label{eq:global_objective}
\end{equation}
where $f_i$ is evaluated locally by agent $i$ and may be nonconvex or simulation-based. This global sum is the evaluation objective used to report terminal fitness in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. During search, each agent instead optimizes the penalized local objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective}; the penalty terms guide consistency but are excluded from reported $F$ values. Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_objective} is therefore the quantity optimized in principle, whereas $h_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective} is the quantity optimized in practice on each agent. Under the distributed setting, this problem is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{X}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{X}_n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(\mathbf{X}_i)
\quad \text{s.t.} \quad x_i^d = x_j^d,\ \forall d\in I_{i,j},\ \forall (i,j)\in E.
\label{eq:distributed_formulation}
\end{equation}
The coupling constraints in Eq.~\eqref{eq:distributed_formulation} are enforced in penalty-based NDO through penalty terms and neighbor negotiation rather than through a central solver. In the negotiation stage, $L_{i,j}$ denotes the shared variables on edge $(i,j)$, i.e., the dimensions indexed by $I_{i,j}$.

\section{Proposed Method}\label{sec:method}
This section presents the proposed conflict-gated distributed optimization architecture for black-box NDO. Each agent maintains a subpopulation, alternates penalized local search with gated neighbor negotiation on shared variables, and updates penalty weights through a modular controller. A three-layer gate decides whether an outer loop should open the negotiation channel. Variable selection and adaptive penalty control refine coordination only after the gate opens. Each agent optimizes
\begin{equation}
h_i(x)=f_i(x)+\alpha_i\, c_i(x)
\label{eq:local_penalty_objective}
\end{equation}
where $f_i(x)$ is the local objective, $c_i(x)$ penalizes deviation from the neighborhood consensus reference on shared dimensions, and $\alpha_i$ weights how strongly inconsistency is punished during local search. Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective} defines the local search objective. It does not decide whether neighbors should communicate in the current outer loop. The architecture therefore separates three decisions: when to communicate (conflict gate), what to negotiate (variable selection), and how strongly to penalize inconsistency (adaptive penalty control).

\subsection{Overall Architecture}\label{sec:pipeline}
The proposed architecture alternates between a local optimization stage and a gated negotiation stage. The local stage uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective} to guide search within each subproblem. The negotiation stage allows neighboring agents to exchange information and enforce consistency on shared variables once communication is granted. Fig.~\ref{fig:proposed_architecture} shows the pipeline. Communication denotes the gate decision; negotiation denotes post-gate processing.

\subsection{Gating Communication with the Conflict Index}\label{sec:gate_design}
\subsubsection{Communication-Timing Formulation}\label{sec:timing_problem}
Let $t=1,2,\ldots,T$ index outer iterations and $t_{\mathrm{last}}$ denote the most recent loop that opened negotiation. At each $t$, agent $i$ chooses a local communication gate $g_i^t\in\{0,1\}$. Here $g_i^t=1$ means that agent $i$ requests negotiation at loop $t$. The global gate is $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=\max_i g_i^t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_gate}). If $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=1$, the neighborhood enters the negotiation branch. If $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=0$, agents skip negotiation, continue local search, and refresh consensus references. An always-on schedule fixes $g_i^t\equiv 1$. The communication-timing problem is to design a policy $\pi$ such that $g_i^t=\pi(\mathcal{H}_i^t)$, where $\mathcal{H}_i^t$ contains observable local history (local best, consensus reference, conflict index, and elapsed loops since $t_{\mathrm{last}}$).

Two equivalent objectives capture the design trade-off. In the budget form, the goal is to minimize terminal global objective $F(\mathbf{X}^T)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_objective} subject to average trigger rate $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T g_{\mathrm{global}}^t \le B$. In the tolerance form, the goal is to minimize $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ subject to $F(\mathbf{X}^T)$ not worse than an always-on baseline by more than tolerance $\delta$. Black-box NDO lacks convexity, so we do not claim global convergence theorems for the full search. Instead, we provide three gate-level guarantees: a fail-safe drift bound on reference staleness between communications (Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe}), a bounded single-round skip suboptimality controlled by the gate threshold (Proposition~\ref{prop:skip}), and a convergence rate for intermittent gated consensus under the standard linearization (Proposition~\ref{prop:intermittent}), together with an empirical skip criterion whose validation is reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsubsection{Gate Design}
Conflict gating decides whether to open communication before paying negotiation cost. The gate uses a black-box conflict index $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ to estimate whether another negotiation round is likely to improve shared consistency enough to justify its cost. For each active shared dimension $d\in\mathcal{D}_i$, let $x_d^t$ be agent $i$'s local best, $r_d^t$ the consensus reference, and $R_d>0$ the search range on $d$. The per-dimension smoothed gap is
\begin{equation}
\delta_d^t = \beta \delta_d^{t-1} + (1-\beta)\frac{|x_d^t-r_d^t|}{R_d},
\label{eq:ci_dim}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_d^t$ is the exponentially smoothed normalized discrepancy on dimension $d$ and $\beta \in (0,1)$ is the smoothing coefficient. The agent-level conflict index is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CI}_i^t=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_i|}\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_i} \delta_d^t,
\label{eq:ci_agent}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}_i$ is the set of active shared dimensions for agent $i$ at outer loop $t$. High $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ indicates that local shared values still disagree with neighborhood references; low $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ suggests limited expected benefit from another negotiation round.

The conflict index $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ is a black-box proxy computed from local bests and consensus references, not a direct gradient estimate. Conflict-detection ideas in penalty-based NDO~\cite{ref_macpo} motivate the proxy design; the gate decision uses $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ci_agent}.

The phase-relative baseline is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t = \gamma \hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^{t-1} + (1-\gamma)\mathrm{CI}_i^t,
\label{eq:ci_ema}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$ is the exponentially moving average (EMA) baseline of recent conflict and $\gamma\in(0,1)$ controls smoothing. The three-layer local gate is
\begin{equation}
g_i^t=\mathbf{1}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t\right],
\label{eq:local_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function and
\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t &: t-t_{\mathrm{last}}\ge \Delta_{\min}, \label{eq:c_int}\\
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t &: \mathrm{CI}_i^t > \lambda\,\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t\ \ \text{or}\ \ t-t_{\mathrm{last}}\ge K, \label{eq:c_thr}\\
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t &: u_i^t < p_{\mathrm{phase}}^t,\quad u_i^t\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1). \label{eq:c_phase}
\end{align}
Layer~1 enforces a minimum interval $\Delta_{\min}$ between negotiations. Layer~2 opens communication when $\mathrm{CI}_i^t > \lambda \hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$, where $\lambda$ is the relative-threshold multiplier, or forces communication after fail-safe period $K$ silent loops. Layer~3 phase-samples communication with probability $p_{\mathrm{phase}}^t$. Global synchronization uses

\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=\max_i g_i^t.
\label{eq:global_gate}
\end{equation}
where $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=1$ if any agent requests communication at loop $t$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ci_gating} summarizes the procedure and Fig.~\ref{fig:gate_policy} shows the per-agent decision flow. Concrete parameter settings are given in Section~\ref{sec:exp_setup}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \footnotesize
  \caption{Conflict-Gated Communication with Phase Sampling}
  \label{alg:ci_gating}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \For{each outer iteration $t$}
    \State Compute $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ and update $\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$
    \State Check $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t$, $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t$ (relative threshold or fail-safe $K$), and $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t$
    \State $g_i^t\gets \mathbf{1}[\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{thr}}^t\land \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{phase}}^t]$
    \State $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t\gets \max_i g_i^t$
    \If{$g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=0$} \State Skip negotiation and refresh consensus reference
    \Else \State Enter negotiation branch \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Structural Bound and Empirical Criterion}\label{sec:structural_results}
We do not prove bounded per-loop drift for local evolutionary search. Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe} instead treats bounded normalized shared-gap growth during silent loops as an engineering assumption and shows what fail-safe timing guarantees if that assumption holds.

\begin{proposition}[Fail-safe consensus-drift bound]\label{prop:failsafe}
Suppose that between any two consecutive communication events, each normalized shared gap $e_d^t=|x_d^t-r_d^t|/R_d$ increases by at most $\varepsilon_d\ge 0$ on every silent outer loop with $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=0$, and that the relative-threshold rule includes fail-safe $t-t_{\mathrm{last}}\ge K$. Here $e_d^t$ measures the disagreement between agent $i$'s local value and the consensus reference on shared dimension $d$, normalized by range $R_d$. Then any silent stretch has length $\tau=t-t_{\mathrm{last}}\le K-1$ before communication is forced, and
\begin{equation}
e_d^{t_{\mathrm{last}}+\tau} \le e_d^{t_{\mathrm{last}}} + (K-1)\,\varepsilon_{\max},
\qquad \varepsilon_{\max}=\max_d \varepsilon_d.
\label{eq:drift_bound}
\end{equation}
If agent $i$ uses a separable conflict penalty $\alpha_i\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_i} e_d^t$, the incremental penalty inflation attributable to silent drift is at most $\alpha_i(K-1)\varepsilon_{\max}|\mathcal{D}_i|$. Fail-safe therefore limits both reference staleness and penalty distortion between negotiations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
This is a structural fail-safe bound under assumed drift, not a convergence theorem for the full workflow. A mechanical corollary is that fail-safe-only scheduling enforces $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}\ge 1/K$.
\end{remark}

\begin{empiricalcriterion}[Low-conflict skip rule]\label{crit:skip}
At outer loop $t$, skip negotiation when for all active agents (i) $\mathrm{CI}_i^t \le \lambda\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$, (ii) overlap variables have moved little since $t_{\mathrm{last}}$, and (iii) the fail-safe rule has not activated. This is the relative-threshold branch of Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_gate}. It is an engineering skip test motivated by event-triggered coordination, not a guarantee that one negotiation round would leave the pure global objective $F$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_objective} unchanged.
\end{empiricalcriterion}

The skip rule above is stated as an engineering test. Proposition~\ref{prop:skip} strengthens it into a bounded statement: on the penalized local objective that the gate actually monitors, the quality forgone by skipping a low-conflict round is controlled linearly by the same threshold $\lambda\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$ that triggers the skip.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded skip suboptimality]\label{prop:skip}
Let agent $i$'s penalized local objective be $h_i(\mathbf{x})=f_i(\mathbf{x})+\alpha_i P_i$, with separable conflict penalty $P_i=\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_i}\phi(\delta_d^t)$ over the smoothed normalized gaps $\delta_d^t$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ci_dim}, where $\phi$ is $L$-Lipschitz with $\phi(0)=0$ (the separable linear penalty of Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe} satisfies this with $L=1$). Assume one negotiation round acts as a non-expansive consensus operator $\mathcal{N}$ on gaps, i.e., $\delta_d(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x})\le \delta_d^t$ for all $d$ (averaging toward consensus references never enlarges a normalized gap). Then the penalty reduction forgone by skipping one round at loop $t$ is nonnegative and bounded by the current conflict:
\begin{equation}
0\le h_i(\mathbf{x}^t)-h_i(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}^t)\le \alpha_i L\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_i} \delta_d^t = \alpha_i L\,|\mathcal{D}_i|\,\mathrm{CI}_i^t,
\label{eq:skip_bound}
\end{equation}
where the last equality is the definition of $\mathrm{CI}_i^t$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ci_agent}. Hence, whenever the relative-threshold skip rule of Empirical Criterion~\ref{crit:skip} holds ($\mathrm{CI}_i^t\le\lambda\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$), the single-round skip suboptimality is linearly controlled by the gate threshold:
\begin{equation}
h_i(\mathbf{x}^t)-h_i(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}^t)\ \le\ \alpha_i L\,|\mathcal{D}_i|\,\lambda\,\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t.
\label{eq:skip_bound_gate}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\phi$ is $L$-Lipschitz, $|\phi(a)-\phi(b)|\le L|a-b|$; with $\delta_d(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x})\le\delta_d^t$ each term obeys $0\le\phi(\delta_d^t)-\phi(\delta_d(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}))\le L\,(\delta_d^t-\delta_d(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}))\le L\,\delta_d^t$. Negotiation leaves the local fitness term $f_i$ unchanged (it only updates shared references), so $h_i(\mathbf{x}^t)-h_i(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}^t)=\alpha_i\sum_d[\phi(\delta_d^t)-\phi(\delta_d(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}))]$. Summing the per-dimension bounds gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:skip_bound}; substituting the skip rule gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:skip_bound_gate}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:skip} upgrades Empirical Criterion~\ref{crit:skip} from an engineering test to a bounded statement: skipping a low-conflict round cannot forgo more penalized-objective improvement than $\alpha_i L|\mathcal{D}_i|\lambda\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^t$, so this quality loss vanishes linearly as conflict falls. It bounds the penalized local objective $h_i$ that the gate monitors, not the pure global objective $F$; together with the fail-safe drift bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe}), the two results limit both what a skip forgoes within a silent stretch and how far reference staleness can grow before communication is forced. The non-expansiveness assumption on $\mathcal{N}$ holds at the system level for averaging, gossip, and best-response negotiation alike; Fig.~\ref{fig:skip_bound} in Section~\ref{sec:exp_comm} verifies the bound numerically across all three and shows $\Delta P\propto\overline{\mathrm{CI}}$, so low-conflict skips forgo vanishing improvement.
\end{remark}

Proposition~\ref{prop:skip} bounds one skipped loop. Chaining it over a full silent stretch, together with the fail-safe horizon and the drift assumption of Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe}, bounds the total quality forgone between two communications.

\begin{corollary}[Cumulative skip suboptimality over a silent stretch]\label{cor:skip_cumulative}
Consider a silent stretch $s=t_{\mathrm{last}},\dots,t_{\mathrm{last}}+\tau-1$ of length $\tau=t-t_{\mathrm{last}}\le K-1$ (guaranteed by fail-safe). Suppose the relative-threshold skip rule holds at every skipped loop, $\mathrm{CI}_i^s\le\lambda\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^s$, and the smoothed gap drifts by at most $\varepsilon_{\max}$ per silent loop (the assumption of Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe}), so that $\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^s\le\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^{t_{\mathrm{last}}}+(s-t_{\mathrm{last}})\varepsilon_{\max}$. Then the total penalized-objective improvement forgone across the stretch is bounded:
\begin{align}
\sum_{s=t_{\mathrm{last}}}^{t_{\mathrm{last}}+\tau-1}\!\!\big[h_i(\mathbf{x}^s)-h_i(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}^s)\big]
&\le \alpha_i L\,|\mathcal{D}_i|\,\lambda\sum_{s}\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^s \nonumber\\
&\hspace{-6.5em}\le\ \alpha_i L\,|\mathcal{D}_i|\,\lambda\Big[(K{-}1)\,\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^{t_{\mathrm{last}}}+\tfrac{(K{-}1)(K{-}2)}{2}\varepsilon_{\max}\Big].
\label{eq:skip_cumulative}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:skip_bound_gate} at each skipped loop $s$ and sum: $\sum_s[h_i(\mathbf{x}^s)-h_i(\mathcal{N}\mathbf{x}^s)]\le\alpha_i L|\mathcal{D}_i|\lambda\sum_s\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^s$. Substituting $\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^s\le\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^{t_{\mathrm{last}}}+(s-t_{\mathrm{last}})\varepsilon_{\max}$ and $\sum_{j=0}^{\tau-1}j=\tau(\tau-1)/2$ with $\tau\le K-1$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:skip_cumulative}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The cumulative cost grows linearly in the threshold $\lambda\hat{\mu}$ and at most quadratically in the fail-safe horizon $K$: a shorter $K$ or smaller $\lambda$ tightens the guarantee, quantifying the communication--quality knob that $(\lambda,K)$ expose. The leading term $\alpha_i L|\mathcal{D}_i|\lambda(K{-}1)\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{CI},i}^{t_{\mathrm{last}}}$ vanishes as conflict falls, so a long silent stretch is cheap precisely when it is triggered by low conflict.
\end{remark}

While Propositions~\ref{prop:failsafe} and~\ref{prop:skip} bound single-loop staleness and skip cost, a natural follow-up question is whether repeatedly skipping still lets shared variables converge. For the standard linearization of the consensus/negotiation step this can be answered exactly, and the answer reuses the gate's own trigger rate.

\begin{proposition}[Convergence of intermittent gated negotiation]\label{prop:intermittent}
Consider the linearized consensus recursion of one negotiation round acting on the disagreement component $\mathbf{e}^t$ (deviation of shared variables from their consensus mean): a communicated round applies $\mathbf{e}^{t+1}=(\mathbf{I}-\lambda_c\mathbf{L})\mathbf{e}^t$, where $\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W}$ is the graph Laplacian of the neighbor mixing matrix $\mathbf{W}$ and $\lambda_c>0$ is the consensus gain, while a skipped round applies $\mathbf{e}^{t+1}=\mathbf{e}^t$. Let $0<\gamma_2\le\gamma_N$ denote the smallest nonzero and the largest eigenvalues of $\mathbf{L}$. If the global gate opens on each loop with probability $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}=p$ independently of the state, then the expected disagreement contracts geometrically:
\begin{equation}
\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{e}^{t}]\|\le \rho(p\lambda_c)^{\,t}\,\|\mathbf{e}^{0}\|,\quad \rho(p\lambda_c)=\max\{|1-p\lambda_c\gamma_2|,\,|1-p\lambda_c\gamma_N|\},
\label{eq:intermittent_rate}
\end{equation}
and $\rho(p\lambda_c)<1$ if and only if $0<p\lambda_c<2/\gamma_N$. Equivalently, gating at trigger rate $p$ is a consensus recursion with effective gain $p\lambda_c$: intermittent negotiation converges in expectation whenever the always-on recursion with gain $p\lambda_c$ does.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The gate indicator is Bernoulli$(p)$ and independent of the state, so the expected one-loop operator is $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}]=p(\mathbf{I}-\lambda_c\mathbf{L})+(1-p)\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{I}-p\lambda_c\mathbf{L}$. On the disagreement subspace $\mathbf{L}$ has eigenvalues in $[\gamma_2,\gamma_N]$, hence $\mathbf{I}-p\lambda_c\mathbf{L}$ has eigenvalues $1-p\lambda_c\mu$ with $\mu\in[\gamma_2,\gamma_N]$, and its spectral radius is $\max_{\mu\in[\gamma_2,\gamma_N]}|1-p\lambda_c\mu|=\rho(p\lambda_c)$, attained at an endpoint. Taking expectations of the recursion and iterating yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:intermittent_rate}; $\rho(p\lambda_c)<1$ requires $|1-p\lambda_c\gamma_N|<1$, i.e.\ $p\lambda_c<2/\gamma_N$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Two consequences match the design goals. First, the Chebyshev gain $\lambda_c^\star=2/(\gamma_2+\gamma_N)$ minimizes $\rho(p\lambda_c)$ for any fixed budget $p$, giving the fastest gated consensus. Second, for small $p\lambda_c$, $1-\rho(p\lambda_c)\approx p\lambda_c\gamma_2$, so progress per communicated round $(1-\rho)/p\approx\lambda_c\gamma_2$ is independent of the trigger rate: the gate buys a $p$-fold communication saving at a proportional, not super-linear, slowdown and therefore does not waste per-message consensus efficiency. This is the convergence-side counterpart of Proposition~\ref{prop:skip}. It concerns the linearized consensus recursion, not global convergence of the black-box search, consistent with the scope stated in Section~\ref{sec:problem}.
\end{remark}

Equation~\eqref{eq:intermittent_rate} controls the first moment $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{e}^t]$. Because a rarely applied but expansive step can shrink the mean while inflating the variance, we also give the stronger mean-square guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Mean-square convergence of intermittent gated negotiation]\label{cor:intermittent_ms}
Assume in addition that $\mathbf{W}$ is symmetric (e.g.\ Metropolis weights), so $\mathbf{L}$ has an orthonormal eigenbasis with eigenvalues $\mu_k\in[\gamma_2,\gamma_N]$ on the disagreement subspace. Under the state-independent Bernoulli$(p)$ gate of Proposition~\ref{prop:intermittent}, the expected squared disagreement contracts geometrically,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{e}^{t}\|^2\big]\le \rho_{\mathrm{ms}}^{\,t}\,\|\mathbf{e}^{0}\|^2,\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{ms}}(p,\lambda_c)=(1-p)+p\,\rho(\lambda_c)^2,
\label{eq:ms_rate}
\end{equation}
and $\rho_{\mathrm{ms}}<1$ if and only if $\rho(\lambda_c)<1$, i.e.\ $0<\lambda_c<2/\gamma_N$, for \emph{any} trigger rate $p\in(0,1]$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The one-loop operator equals $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{I}-\lambda_c\mathbf{L}$ with probability $p$ and $\mathbf{I}$ with probability $1-p$, independently of the state, so $\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{e}^{t+1}\|^2\mid\mathbf{e}^t]=p\|\mathbf{M}\mathbf{e}^t\|^2+(1-p)\|\mathbf{e}^t\|^2$. Expanding $\mathbf{e}^t=\sum_k c_k\mathbf{v}_k$ in the eigenbasis of $\mathbf{L}$ gives $\|\mathbf{M}\mathbf{e}^t\|^2=\sum_k(1-\lambda_c\mu_k)^2c_k^2$, hence $\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{e}^{t+1}\|^2\mid\mathbf{e}^t]=\sum_k[p(1-\lambda_c\mu_k)^2+(1-p)]c_k^2\le[\max_k p(1-\lambda_c\mu_k)^2+(1-p)]\|\mathbf{e}^t\|^2$. The bracket is convex in $\mu_k$ and thus maximized at an endpoint $\mu\in\{\gamma_2,\gamma_N\}$, giving $\rho_{\mathrm{ms}}=(1-p)+p\,\rho(\lambda_c)^2$. Taking total expectations and iterating yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:ms_rate}; $\rho_{\mathrm{ms}}<1\Leftrightarrow p\,\rho(\lambda_c)^2<p\Leftrightarrow\rho(\lambda_c)<1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Mean-square convergence is strictly stronger than the first-moment result and its condition is cleaner: it needs only a contractive per-communication step, $\lambda_c<2/\gamma_N$, independent of $p$, whereas the first-moment operator requires $p\lambda_c<2/\gamma_N$. The per-round mean-square progress $(1-\rho_{\mathrm{ms}})/p=1-\rho(\lambda_c)^2$ is again independent of the trigger rate, so the ``no wasted communication'' property of Proposition~\ref{prop:intermittent} holds in the second moment as well.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Expected Communication--Quality Trade-off}
Under gate $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t$, expected negotiation cost scales as $\mathcal{O}(I\,\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}\,\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}\sum_{(i,j)}|L_{i,j}|)$ versus $\mathcal{O}(I\sum_{(i,j)}|L_{i,j}|)$ for an always-on schedule, where $I$ is the number of outer iterations and $\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}$ is the fraction of shared dimensions actively negotiated after gating. The tunable parameters $(\lambda,K,p_{\mathrm{phase}})$ trace a trade-off surface between communication rate and terminal fitness. The empirical shape of this surface is reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp_comm}.

\subsection{Variable Selection for Negotiation}\label{sec:selection}
Once communication is granted, shared dimensions can be ranked by importance score $q_d^t$ and updated by an EMA rule over conflict contribution and negotiation effectiveness, where $q_d^t$ measures how likely dimension $d$ is to move the local frontier if negotiated. Low-impact dimensions may be skipped so that negotiation concentrates on coordinates with the highest expected coordination value.

\subsection{Negotiation}\label{sec:negotiation}
If $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=1$, neighboring agents negotiate on shared variables through evaluation, competition, and sharing (Algorithm~\ref{alg:negotiation_consistency}). Concretely, neighbors exchange local-best candidates, perform Nash-style bilateral comparison on active shared dimensions, apply two-sided perturbation consistency checking, and write accepted values back to the consensus reference. This branch is where cross-agent consistency is enforced after gate activation.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \footnotesize
  \caption{Negotiation with Two-Sided Consistency Check}
  \label{alg:negotiation_consistency}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Neighbor pair $(i,j)$, shared set $L_{i,j}$, dimension flags $\{z_d\}$
  \For{each shared dimension $d\in L_{i,j}$ with $z_d=1$}
    \State Nash-style bilateral comparison on $d$
    \State Two-sided perturbation check and update $z_d$ if consistent
    \State Write accepted values to consensus reference
  \EndFor
  \State Re-evaluate local population
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Penalty-Based Local Optimization}\label{sec:penalty_interface}
The conflict term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective} is
\begin{equation}
c_i(x)=\sum_{d\in\Theta_i}\frac{\left|x^d-x_{i,\mathrm{con}}^d\right|}{u_d-l_d},
\label{eq:conflict_term}
\end{equation}
where $x_{i,\mathrm{con}}^d$ is the consensus reference for dimension $d$ and $[l_d,u_d]$ is its box bound. Penalty strength adapts through a modular interface that accepts EMA-smoothed, fixed-phase, or RL-based controllers without altering the communication architecture. All controllers output a bounded penalty ratio $\rho_i$ and map it to the penalty weight through
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i^{t+1}=\bar{\alpha}_i^{t+1}\rho_i^{t+1},
\label{eq:alpha_mapping}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\alpha}_i$ is an objective-scale baseline penalty and $\rho_i\in[\rho_{\min},\rho_{\max}]$ is the bounded penalty ratio that provides online adaptation of $\alpha_i$. Under the RL-based default, a lightweight policy observes conflict level and trend, updates $\rho_i$, and thereby adjusts $\alpha_i$. EMA and fixed schedules plug into the same mapping. The interface does not replace the embedded local optimizer. Controller alternatives and the RL-based default used in the complete configuration are compared in Section~\ref{sec:exp_ablations}.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Event-triggered and communication-efficient optimization.}
Reducing inter-agent transmissions is an active research line in distributed optimization and federated learning. Periodic local SGD and FedAvg remain the default schedules but ignore online feedback about whether another round would help~\cite{ref_stich_local_sgd_2019,ref_mcmahan_fedavg_2017,ref_koloskova_decsgd_2020}. Recent federated-learning work instead adapts synchronization from gradient consistency~\cite{ref_gift_fl_2023} or prunes model updates in decentralized device-to-device edge networks~\cite{ref_dfl_prune_tnse_2025}. In control and sensor networks, event-triggered distributed optimization transmits only when a local error exceeds a dynamic threshold~\cite{ref_et_nonconvex_tac_2023}, and asynchronous event-triggered schemes further tolerate heterogeneous computation and intermittent links~\cite{ref_daaet_tac_2024}. These methods cut communication substantially, but their trigger signals are gradient-based, model-based, or consensus-error-based. In black-box network-based distributed optimization (NDO), objectives are locally evaluable yet gradient-uncomputable, so the above triggers cannot be applied directly. The gap motivates a communication gate driven by a gradient-free conflict proxy rather than by unavailable gradient or model error.

\paragraph{Penalty-based cooperative evolution for black-box NDO.}
For gradient-uncomputable NDO, penalty-based cooperative evolution remains the most practical paradigm~\cite{ref_potter_dejong,ref_cc_dist_accel_2024}. MACPO~\cite{ref_macpo} is a representative solver: each agent maintains a subpopulation, optimizes a penalized local objective, and negotiates shared variables with neighbors. MASOIE~\cite{ref_masoie_2024} and MAES-CCSA~\cite{ref_maes_ccsa_2025} extend this line with adaptive internal and external learning and cooperative step adaptation for large-scale black-box distributed search. Cooperative coevolution analyses~\cite{ref_cc_dist_accel_2024} further study convergence and distributed acceleration in non-separable large-scale settings. These recent methods advance search quality and scalability, yet their negotiation schedules are still always-on or fixed-period: they negotiate on nearly every outer loop or on a preset cadence without asking whether the current local conflict justifies another transmission. None provides an explicit when-to-communicate gate for shared-variable negotiation under a communication budget. Our work targets that missing decision layer while remaining in the MACPO penalty-negotiation setting.

\paragraph{RL for online parameter adaptation.}
Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a routine tool for online parameter adaptation in metaheuristics and evolutionary computation~\cite{ref_karafotias_param_control,ref_rl_ea_survey_2024,ref_rl_metaheuristic_2023}. Recent surveys document RL-assisted evolutionary algorithms as an established research direction~\cite{ref_rl_ea_survey_2024}, and gradient-informed RL controllers have been used to adapt metaheuristic parameters during search~\cite{ref_rl_metaheuristic_2023}. In multi-agent and distributed settings, RL is likewise used to tune coordination hyperparameters rather than to replace the underlying optimizer~\cite{ref_sutton_barto}. In our architecture, RL plays exactly that auxiliary role: after the conflict gate opens, a lightweight policy adjusts the penalty ratio $\rho_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_mapping}. RL does not decide whether to communicate and does not replace evolutionary local search, which keeps the primary contribution on interpretable communication timing.

\paragraph{Communication-constrained physical applications.}
The communication bottleneck is not abstract in recent application studies. Distributed optimization tutorials for multi-robot and UAV systems emphasize that wireless coordination can dominate energy use relative to on-board computation~\cite{ref_dopt_robotics_2024}. Fully distributed task offloading in vehicular edge computing must cope with brief contact windows and limited vehicular bandwidth~\cite{ref_dopt_iov_2024}. In power systems, distributed secondary control for islanded DC microgrids~\cite{ref_dopt_smartgrid_2024} and random-triggered economic dispatch over wide-area links~\cite{ref_ndo_dispatch_2024} both treat transmission rate as a first-order design constraint. Because bandwidth, energy, and latency limits are central in these robot, vehicular, and grid settings, a conflict-gated negotiation schedule is not only an algorithmic refinement but also an engineering requirement. The proposed architecture addresses that requirement by coupling a relative conflict-index gate with a fail-safe drift bound, enabling direct trade-off control between communication budget and terminal fitness in black-box NDO.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
This section evaluates the proposed method, namely the conflict-gated architecture in Section~\ref{sec:method} with conflict gating, variable selection, and RL-based adaptive penalty control, against an always-on MACPO baseline~\cite{ref_macpo}. In tables, the proposed method is labeled Proposed; it corresponds to the RL-MACPO implementation built on the MACPO software lineage. MACPO denotes the Multi-Agent Co-evolutionary algorithm with Penalty-based Objective~\cite{ref_macpo}: each agent maintains a subpopulation, searches with Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective}, and negotiates with neighbors on shared variables under an always-on schedule ($g_i^t\equiv 1$). Unless stated otherwise, complete refers to the proposed default configuration and penalty-only denotes adaptive penalty control without gating.

\paragraph{Evaluation objective.} All main results report the best-so-far global objective $F(\mathbf{X})$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_objective}, evaluated on the assembled decision vector at the end of the allocated evaluation budget. During search each agent optimizes the penalized objective $h_i=f_i+\alpha_i c_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_penalty_objective}; the penalty terms $\alpha_i c_i(\cdot)$ guide consistency but are excluded from reported $F$. Concretely, $F=\sum_i f_i(\mathbf{X}_i)$ counts only local black-box costs, not overlap penalties. The MACPO implementation logs this penalty-free quantity separately from the penalized fitness used during search; we refer to it only as $F$ in the paper, not by any internal variable name. Lower $F$ is better unless a caption states otherwise. Proposed and MACPO follow the evaluation protocol in~\cite{ref_macpo}; on F7--F18, MACPO$^{\ddagger}$ entries are archived endpoint logs that may still carry residual penalty when negotiation fails (Table~\ref{tab:macpo_rl_mean_f1_f18}).

We first report settings, then mechanism ablations, baseline comparison, communication efficiency, and application pilots.

\subsection{Experiment Settings}\label{sec:exp_setup}
In general, a larger local subproblem needs more generations to search its dimension space. For comparability with prior NDO studies, the number of local generations in one outer loop is set to $k=0.4D_i$, where $D_i=|\Theta_i|$ is the local dimension of agent $i$~\cite{ref_macpo}. The per-agent fitness-evaluation cap is $3000\times D_i$. Optimization stops once any agent reaches this cap. Each configuration is repeated 25 times. We run 25 paired independent trials for the proposed method and the always-on baseline with matched random seeds. \subsubsection{Architecture and gating ablations}\label{sec:q3_modules}
We next ablate gating depth, variable selection, phase sampling, and penalty-update knobs while holding the rest of the complete configuration fixed.

\paragraph{Conflict gating.} Without Layer~2 (relative threshold), gating collapses variance on F1 (Fig.~\ref{fig:gating_ablation}). Configurations without gating and with Layer~1 only exceed MACPO with coefficient of variation up to 17.6\%. Layers~1+2 and the complete gate restore the target mean (about $6.3\times10^6$) with low variance. Layer~2 is necessary; Layer~3 has marginal effect.

\paragraph{Variable selection.} The complete configuration ranks shared dimensions by $q_d^t$ and negotiates only a phase-dependent subset (Section~\ref{sec:method}; ablations disable this filter). Together with gating, this concentrates negotiation on high-impact dimensions rather than all overlaps every round. The F1--F18 gains therefore reflect the gated-and-filtered architecture, not penalty choice alone.

\paragraph{Phase sampling and $\eta$ strategies.} Ablations with and without phase sampling, together with four $\eta$ strategies, show nearly identical final $F$ on F1--F6 once Layer~2 gating is active. These knobs mainly redistribute communication timing rather than change endpoint quality.

\paragraph{Communication analysis.} With defaults in Table~\ref{tab:gate_defaults}, most outer loops fail the relative threshold and communication occurs mainly through minimum interval, fail-safe $K{=}10$, and phase sampling, yielding about one negotiation every twelve outer loops on F3--F18. F1/F2 stay above this floor because sustained conflict crosses the relative threshold more often. Fig.~\ref{fig:comm_fitness_pareto} places Proposed closer to the lower-left communication-budget frontier than periodic-$K$ pilots on F1/F2/F5. Fig.~\ref{fig:ci_bin_trigger} shows trigger probability falling from the lowest conflict quintile to the next two quintiles on F1--F6, consistent with Empirical Criterion~\ref{crit:skip}.

\paragraph{Protocol-agnostic gating.} The conflict gate reads only a black-box conflict index and a trigger signal, so its benefit should not depend on the specific negotiation rule that follows. We test this in a solver-agnostic consensus sandbox where the same three-layer gate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_gate} drives three structurally different negotiation rules: symmetric Metropolis averaging, randomized pairwise gossip, and an asymmetric best-response update in which only agents whose gap exceeds the population median move toward their neighborhood reference. Agents receive sparse, unpredictable conflict shocks, and we sweep the fail-safe period $K$ to trace the achievable trigger-rate versus steady consensus-error frontier, comparing against periodic and random schedules at matched budget. Fig.~\ref{fig:gated_universality} shows that across all three rules the gate attains an error-budget trade-off at or below both baselines in the scarce-communication regime that the paper operates in, event-triggered communication concentrates the budget on the shocks that raise conflict, whereas time-triggered schedules of equal average rate spend it on quiet loops. This supports the claim that gating is a communication-scheduling layer whose "skip when conflict is low" property transfers across negotiation paradigms, and it instantiates the non-expansive operator $\mathcal{N}$ assumed in Proposition~\ref{prop:skip}.

\paragraph{Skip-bound validation.} Proposition~\ref{prop:skip} bounds the penalized-objective improvement forgone by skipping one negotiation round by $L|\mathcal{D}_i|\,\mathrm{CI}_i^t$. Summed over agents against the consensus mean, the aggregate penalty $P=\sum_{i,d}|x_{i,d}-\bar{x}_d|/R_d$ is non-expansive for all three rules, including randomized gossip: a single pairwise average obeys $|x_i-\bar{x}|+|x_j-\bar{x}|\ge 2|m-\bar{x}|$ by the triangle inequality, and a full sweep composes such steps. Fig.~\ref{fig:skip_bound} tests the system-level bound over thousands of visited states: applying one negotiation round and recording the realized penalty drop $\Delta P$ against the mean conflict $\overline{\mathrm{CI}}$, the bound holds in $100\%$ of cases for Metropolis averaging, randomized gossip, and best-response, with $\Delta P$ strongly proportional to $\overline{\mathrm{CI}}$ (correlation $0.89$--$0.95$). The improvement given up by a skip thus shrinks linearly to zero as conflict falls, across all three structurally different negotiation rules, which is exactly why skipping low-conflict rounds is safe.

\paragraph{Intermittent-consensus convergence.} Proposition~\ref{prop:intermittent} predicts the first-moment decay rate $\rho(p\lambda_c)$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:intermittent_ms} the mean-square rate $\rho_{\mathrm{ms}}=(1-p)+p\,\rho(\lambda_c)^2$. Fig.~\ref{fig:gated_convergence} tests both on an ill-conditioned ring and a random graph: the measured first-moment ($\|\mathbb{E}\mathbf{e}\|$) and mean-square ($\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{e}\|^2$) decay rates of Bernoulli-gated consensus track their predictions across the whole trigger-rate range (maximum absolute deviations $0.004$ and $0.008$). The near-linear rise of both rates as $p$ shrinks is the proportional slowdown of the remarks after Proposition~\ref{prop:intermittent} and Corollary~\ref{cor:intermittent_ms}: halving communication does not more-than-halve consensus progress in either moment, so gating preserves per-message efficiency rather than destabilizing negotiation.

\subsection{Cross-Paradigm Gating on MASOIE}\label{sec:exp_masoie_gated}
The sandbox and the linearized analysis argue that the gate is protocol-agnostic; we now attach it to a real solver whose negotiation is structurally unlike MACPO. MASOIE~\cite{ref_masoie_2024} coordinates subswarms not through a penalty but through an \emph{external-learning velocity} that, on every outer loop, pulls each agent toward its neighbors' solutions. We insert the three-layer conflict gate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_gate} before this exchange-and-external-learning step, so negotiation runs only when the conflict index crosses the relative threshold or the fail-safe fires. A global lockstep decision $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t=\max_i g_i^t$ computed by one all-reduce keeps the message passing deadlock-free. The always-on and gated arms use the same binary and evaluation budget, isolating the effect of the gate.

Table~\ref{tab:masoie_gated} reports F1--F6 (25-dimensional, 20 agents, 10 runs). On F2 and F4, whose always-on runs reach near-exact consensus (disagreement $\sim\!10^{-4}$ and $\sim\!10^{-6}$), the default gate is too aggressive and fitness degrades.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper addressed when to negotiate under a communication budget in distributed black-box optimization by presenting a conflict-gated distributed optimization architecture. The central design separates whether to communicate from how to negotiate: a three-layer gate driven by an online conflict proxy, a phase-relative threshold, and a fail-safe bound skips low-value negotiation rounds while penalized local search and gated neighbor negotiation carry out coordination once communication is granted.

Experiments show that the proposed architecture preserves or improves final fitness under the same evaluation budget while cutting negotiation triggers to a small fraction of outer loops. Mechanism studies further show that the communication gate, rather than penalty-controller choice, dominates the gain. Application pilots indicate that the same timing mechanism transfers to dispatch and transmission-network cases under bandwidth limits.

Beyond these empirical results, the gate is placed on a theoretical footing by three guarantees, each paired with a validity experiment: a fail-safe drift bound on reference staleness, a bounded single-round skip suboptimality that is linear in the gate threshold and accumulates over a fail-safe-bounded silent stretch, and a convergence rate for intermittent gated consensus in both the first moment and the mean square, showing that gating at trigger rate $p$ behaves as a consensus recursion with effective gain $p\lambda_c$ while preserving per-message efficiency. The gate is moreover protocol-agnostic: it plugs unchanged into structurally different negotiation rules and into the real external-learning solver MASOIE, where the communication--quality trade-off it exposes is governed, as the theory predicts, by each problem's consensus sensitivity.

The work still has limitations. Internal comparisons use a shared experimental platform for fair baseline reproduction; the cross-paradigm study of Section~\ref{sec:exp_masoie_gated} attaches the gate to the real external-learning solver MASOIE on 20 agents rather than the full MACPO configuration, and MAES-CCSA remains unavailable in open source. Proposition~\ref{prop:failsafe} assumes bounded per-loop shared drift rather than proving it for the embedded local optimizer. Propositions~\ref{prop:skip}--\ref{prop:intermittent} and Corollary~\ref{cor:skip_cumulative} bound the penalized local objective $h_i$ and the linearized consensus recursion, not the pure global objective $F$ of the full black-box search, and the cumulative bound covers a fail-safe-bounded silent stretch rather than an entire run. On consensus-critical functions (F2/F4) the default gate must be softened to a higher trigger rate. IEEE~118 shows a detectable cost offset, and asynchronous or time-varying networks remain open.

Future work includes fuller OPF-constrained dispatch benchmarks, learned policies for topology-level timing decisions, and matched-budget comparisons once MAES-CCSA code is available.\label{sec:future_rl} A natural extension is to apply learning directly to communication scheduling, selecting neighbors, rounds, or subgraphs, where hand-crafted rules may be insufficient.

\section{Complexity and Cost Analysis}\label{sec:complexity}\label{sec:exec_cost}
This appendix is an \emph{empirical execution-cost model}, not a worst-case complexity proof. Under an always-on schedule, MACPO incurs negotiation work on every outer loop, so per-run negotiation cost scales with $I\sum_{(i,j)}|L_{i,j}|C_f$ up to implementation constants. The proposed method scales that cost by how often the gate opens and how many shared dimensions enter negotiation.

Let $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ and $\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}$ denote time averages within a run:
\begin{equation}
\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}=\frac{1}{I}\sum_{t=1}^{I} g_{\mathrm{global}}^t,
\qquad
\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}=\frac{1}{I}\sum_{t=1}^{I}\frac{|\{d:z_d=1\}|}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{shared}}|},
\label{eq:empirical_averages}
\end{equation}
where $I$ is the number of outer loops, $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t$ is from Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_gate}, and the second term averages the fraction of shared dimensions actively negotiated after selection. Phase sampling makes both quantities random along a run; we average them over independent runs rather than claiming a worst-case bound.

Under these definitions, expected per-run negotiation cost is modeled as
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{nego}}^{\mathrm{Gated}}\right]
=
\mathcal{O}\!\left(
I\,\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}\,\bar r_{\mathrm{act}}
\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|L_{i,j}|\,C_f
\right)+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{penalty}}),
\label{eq:nego_complexity}
\end{equation}
where $|L_{i,j}|$ is the number of shared dimensions on edge $(i,j)$, $C_f$ is the per-dimension negotiation cost, and $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{penalty}}$ is optional penalty-controller overhead. Multiplying by $\bar p_{\mathrm{comm}}$ encodes that negotiation is paid only when $g_{\mathrm{global}}^t{=}1$; measured trigger rates appear in Table~\ref{tab:comm_rate_f1_f18}.
