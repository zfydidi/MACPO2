# SOTA Gap Map

## Scope and Evidence Rule

This map is limited to locally verifiable material indexed in
`reference_materials/source_index.md`, principally `conference_new_ready.tex`,
`confirmed_contribution.md`, `confirmed_motivation.md`,
`results_validation.md`, `experiment_summary_zh.tex`, the local experiment
summaries under `media/`, and the nine PDFs under `references_intro/`.
Statements in the manuscript about prior work are treated as positioning that
still requires citation-level verification; they are not treated as independent
experimental evidence. Numerical claims below are bounded by the conditions
recorded in the local results-validation map.

| Candidate Contribution | What SOTA Already Does | User Evidence | Real Gap | Claim Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1. A transparent three-layer conflict gate for deciding when black-box NDO agents negotiate.** The gate combines a minimum interval, a phase-relative conflict threshold, and fail-safe synchronization. | The manuscript's locally cited positioning says gradient-based distributed optimization, federated learning, and event-triggered optimization already reduce communication using gradient consistency, update pruning, error thresholds, or asynchronous triggers. Penalty-based cooperative evolution and MACPO already coordinate edge-coupled/shared variables, but MACPO is represented locally as using an almost always-on negotiation schedule. | `conference_new_ready.tex` defines the gate and algorithm. Gate ablations in `results_validation.md` report that the full relative-threshold/fail-safe variant reduces trigger rate by about 46% versus the always-on variant on F1/F2/F5 under 30-run LLSO tests; the same map identifies the relative-threshold layer as the mechanism needed for stable sparse negotiation. | The defensible gap is not "communication-efficient optimization is absent." It is the lack, in the locally assembled comparison set, of an interpretable online scheduler for **explicit shared-variable negotiation** in gradient-free, penalty-based black-box NDO. The gap is narrower than general event-triggered optimization. | **High for the method and internal mechanism; Moderate for novelty relative to all SOTA.** | Citation-level novelty must be checked against event-triggered black-box/distributed evolutionary optimization. Avoid saying this is the first communication gate unless the citation audit establishes it. The distinction between exchanging gradients/consensus states and scheduling negotiation must be stated precisely. |
| **C2. Separation of “whether to communicate” from “how to negotiate,” with RL confined to post-gate penalty adaptation.** | Existing local manuscript positioning includes communication scheduling methods and adaptive algorithm-parameter control, including RL-based parameter control. The current paper also acknowledges that EMA, fixed-phase, and RL penalty controllers can produce similar results once the gate is fixed. | `conference_new_ready.tex` explicitly places the non-RL gate before negotiation and applies RL only after communication is granted. `confirmed_contribution.md` calls this an interpretable timing layer. `results_validation.md` and `experiment_summary_zh.tex` state that controller choice is secondary to the gate and that RL/EMA/fixed schedules are statistically tied in the relevant ablation. | The real contribution is an architectural decomposition that makes the communication decision auditable and protocol-facing while leaving penalty adaptation modular. It is **not** evidence that RL is superior, nor a new RL algorithm. | **High for architectural separation; Low for an RL novelty claim.** | Reviewers may view this as system decomposition rather than an algorithmic advance. Any title, abstract, or contribution statement centered on RL would overstate the evidence. Retain RL as an implementation module, not the paper's novelty anchor. |
| **C3. Gate-level analytical guarantees: fail-safe drift, bounded skipped-round loss on the penalized local objective, and intermittent consensus rates under linearization.** | Event-triggered and intermittent-consensus literature already studies thresholds, communication/convergence trade-offs, and consensus rates. The local manuscript does not establish that bounded-drift or intermittent-consensus analysis is absent from SOTA. | `conference_new_ready.tex` contains the three stated propositions and explicitly limits their scope. `results_validation.md` records numerical checks: all tested skip-bound points lie below the bound with correlation 0.89-0.95, and linearized intermittent-consensus experiments report maximum deviations of 0.004/0.008 from theory. The skip result concerns the penalized local objective \(h_i\), not the pure global objective \(F\). The consensus result concerns a linearized recursion, not MACPO/MASOIE convergence. The drift result is conditional. Do not label the package a global convergence theory. |
| **C4. Large communication-trigger savings at matched evaluation budget while preserving or improving terminal benchmark fitness.** | Always-on MACPO supplies the principal same-codebase baseline. The local materials also mention fixed-period/random schedules and external cooperative-evolution solvers, but not all are matched implementations or matched-budget comparisons. Communication-efficient methods broadly aim at the same efficiency goal in other paradigms. | `results_validation.md` records 25 paired runs for F1-F18, terminal objective comparisons against MACPO under the same evaluation cap, and trigger rates near 8% for many F1-F18 cases (about 90% fewer triggered negotiations). It cautions that F1/F2 trigger more often and that not every function should be called a win without checking the table. `experiment_summary_zh.tex` reports a 3.2% difference in cumulative evaluations for the F1-F6 aggregate. | The real empirical gap is evidence that an interpretable conflict gate can exploit redundant always-on negotiation in the authors' black-box NDO platform without paying a terminal-fitness penalty on most tested benchmarks under approximately matched evaluation budgets. | **High for the tested benchmark protocol; Moderate for generalization.** | A trigger is not a byte, packet, energy, or latency measurement. "Up to 90%" must not become an across-the-board average. Large F9/F15 differences may reflect unresolved penalty inconsistency and/or metric-definition issues; the manuscript must ensure both methods compare the same terminal quantity. "Matched budget" should be described with the observed evaluation-count tolerance, not as exact equality if it is not exact. |
| **C5. Protocol-agnostic use of the same gate with averaging, randomized gossip, and simplified best-response negotiation.** | Averaging, gossip, best-response, event-triggered consensus, and periodic/random communication rules are established mechanisms. Applying a common outer scheduler to them is therefore a transfer/ablation question, not evidence that the negotiation rules themselves are new. | `results_validation.md` points to the `gated_universality` sandbox and states that a K-sweep compares the same gate across the three abstract rules under matched budgets; it reports favorable frontiers in scarce-communication regimes but explicitly excludes universal superiority for weak single-round gossip at all rates. `confirmed_contribution.md` lists this as evidence that the gate is independent of a particular negotiation protocol. | The defensible gap is an experimentally demonstrated **scheduling interface** that can sit above structurally different negotiation operators, supporting the claim that the contribution is broader than a MACPO-specific code patch. | **Moderate.** | The evidence comes from a solver-agnostic sandbox, not full deployments of three mature algorithms. "Protocol-agnostic" should mean unchanged gate interface/logic in the tested operators, not guaranteed compatibility with every topology, asynchronous protocol, or time-varying network. |
| **C6. It is not a new baseline created by this paper, and the local materials do not support a universal superiority claim over MASOIE. | `results_validation.md` records 10-run, 20-agent experiments: on 4/6 functions the inserted gate yields a large communication drop while fitness matches or improves; default settings fail on F2/F4; tightening \(K\) recovers the consensus-critical operating point. `confirmed_contribution.md` cites the MASOIE table and recovery figure as cross-paradigm evidence. | The real gap addressed is whether negotiation timing can transfer outside penalty-based MACPO. The mixed result is useful evidence: the gate transfers, but its safe operating point depends on consensus demand. | **Moderate to High for tested transfer; Low for universal plug-and-play claims.** | Only six functions and 10 runs are recorded locally. Default-gate failures must remain prominent. If the MASOIE comparison is not a fully matched reproduction, call it integration/transfer evidence rather than a definitive head-to-head SOTA comparison. Parameter recovery via \(K\) can be criticized as post-hoc tuning unless the selection rule is documented. |
| **C7. The current local record does not establish that the proposed pilots meet full industrial OPF or deployment standards. | `results_validation.md` records 25 paired-run dispatch pilots with 41-88% trigger reduction and broadly matched fitness, including a scenario-specific \(K\) override for IEEE-118. They do not establish a new power-systems solver or deployment-ready communication protocol. | **Moderate as a transfer study; Low as an application-SOTA claim.** | Reviewers may reject simplified simulators as insufficient for a journal application contribution. Avoid claims about real bandwidth, energy savings, OPF feasibility, latency, security, asynchronous links, or industrial deployment without measurements. Report the IEEE-118 offset, outliers, and scenario-specific \(K\) transparently. |
| **C8. A communication-quality operating surface controlled by \((\lambda,K,p_{\mathrm{phase}})\), with conflict-conditioned triggering rather than a fixed period alone.** | Fixed-period, random, and event-triggered schedules already expose communication-quality trade-offs. Thus, the existence of a trade-off surface is not novel by itself. | The manuscript defines budget and tolerance formulations and identifies \((\lambda,K,p_{\mathrm{phase}})\) as controls. `experiment_summary_zh.tex` states that periodic-2/3/5 comparisons on F1/F2/F5 place the proposed method in a favorable low-communication/low-objective region and that low-conflict bins trigger less often. A journal version should explain how users choose \(\lambda\) and \(K\) without benchmark-specific tuning. |

## Gap Summary

1. **Primary defensible gap.** The strongest contribution is an interpretable
   scheduling layer for deciding when to perform explicit shared-variable
   negotiation in distributed, gradient-free, black-box NDO. The local material
   supports a careful distinction from methods that schedule gradients, model
   updates, or consensus states. It does not yet justify a field-wide "first"
   claim.

2. **Best evidence chain.** The most coherent journal claim is:
   three-layer conflict gate -> gate ablation -> matched/approximately matched
   F1-F18 benchmark evidence -> trigger reduction -> analytical scope checks ->
   transfer across abstract negotiation rules and MASOIE. This chain directly
   addresses the reviewer objection that the method is only a MACPO patch.

3. **Theory boundary.** The analytical contribution is useful only when kept
   gate-level. The skip bound is on \(h_i\), the drift result is conditional, and
   the consensus rate is for a linearized model. None supports global convergence
   or global optimality of the full black-box search.

4. **Empirical boundary.** The strongest supported efficiency metric is
   negotiation-trigger rate, not network bytes, energy, wall-clock latency, or
   monetary communication cost. Application pilots support relevance but not an
   industrial deployment claim.

5. **Transfer boundary.** Protocol-agnosticity is supported as an interface
   claim for the tested averaging, gossip, best-response, and MASOIE settings.
   It must not be expanded to arbitrary asynchronous, directed, time-varying, or
   adversarial networks.

6. **Most important journal risks.** The manuscript needs citation-level novelty
   verification against event-triggered black-box/distributed evolutionary
   optimization; consistent endpoint metrics in the largest benchmark gains;
   transparent disclosure of MASOIE F2/F4 failures and \(K\)-based recovery; and
   a principled parameter-selection explanation. Until those checks are complete,
   the safest overall novelty claim is **a new, interpretable conflict-gated
   scheduling architecture with bounded gate-level behavior and multi-setting
   validation**, rather than universal SOTA superiority.
